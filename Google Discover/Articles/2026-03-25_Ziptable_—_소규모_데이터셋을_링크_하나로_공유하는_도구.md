How Ziptable works

Ziptable keeps your data in the browser. No upload. No storage. No sign-up. Just a link.

### What it does

Ziptable lets you share a small CSV or JSON dataset by sending a single link. The person you send it to opens the link and immediately sees the data in their browser, ready to search, inspect, and download again. **No attachments, no cloud storage workflow, and no account required.**

### How the link works

When you open or paste data, Ziptable compresses it and encodes it directly into the URL — specifically into the part after the `#`. That part is called the [[URL fragment|URL fragment]]. **Browsers do not send the fragment to the server.** When someone opens your link, their browser unpacks the data locally and displays it.

### Privacy

Because your data lives in the URL fragment, **Ziptable's server does not receive it when the page loads.** The server only receives a request for the app itself. Your data stays in the browser and in the link you choose to share.

### What about the short link?

The "Shorten URL" option sends the full link to a third-party shortening service. That can make sharing easier, but it also introduces another service into the chain. **The full Ziptable link is the most durable version.**

### Best for small datasets

Ziptable works best for small-to-medium datasets: examples, demos, source tables, and quick shares. Very large datasets create long URLs that some apps may cut off. The compression meter on the main page shows whether your dataset is still in a comfortable sharing range.

### Free and lightweight

**No ads, no sign-up wall, no storage layer to manage.** Ziptable runs entirely in the browser and can still **work offline** once the page has loaded.
