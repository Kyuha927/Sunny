## 🚀 What *Clawdbot* Was

**Clawdbot** was an open-source AI agent project that let people build *persistent, autonomous AI assistants* that could do real tasks — not just chat.

- It ran locally on your hardware and connected to things like email, calendars, smart home devices, and messaging platforms. citeturn0search19  
- Developers and hobbyists could extend its functionality with plugins (“skills”). citeturn0search21  
- It rapidly became *one of the most popular open-source AI projects* in early 2026, gaining tens of thousands of GitHub stars and widespread discussion. citeturn0search19  

---

## 🍤 Name Chaos — *Clawdbot → Moltbot → OpenClaw*

Here’s where the “Google / trademark issue” comes in:

1. **Originally** the project was called **Clawdbot**. citeturn0search19  
2. **Anthropic** — the company behind the “Claude” AI models — raised trademark concerns, because “Clawd” was close to their brand. citeturn0news16  
3. The creator reluctantly renamed the project **Moltbot**, then quickly **renamed it again to OpenClaw** to avoid confusion. citeturn0search19  

So *it wasn’t Google that directly “killed” it by sending a takedown notice* — that was Anthropic’s trademark enforcement. Google comes up in *one* popular story because the original author wrote about how a Google account used for integrations got suspended by Google’s bot-detection systems. citeturn0view0

## 🔥 What *Trond Wuellner’s* Article Actually Describes

The piece you linked (from **trond.ai**) is an essay by a developer who built his own AI assistant using OpenClaw. He explains:

- How the assistant was genuinely useful (managing calendar, emails, smart home, etc.). citeturn0view0  
- That his **Google account got suspended** because Google’s systems detected automated access as suspicious — which broke his integrations. citeturn0view0  
- That current cloud services simply *aren’t built* for autonomous AI agents that act on your behalf. citeturn0view0  

So when he says *“Google killed mine,”* he means **Google’s bot-detection systems flagged the account**, not that Google issued a legal takedown of the project.

## 👀 Broader Problems People Have Seen With OpenClaw/Clawdbot

Beyond naming and API issues, the project threw up other real challenges:

### ⚠️ **Security and Safety Risks**
- Because skills/plugins could access your file system and network, malicious or poorly written extensions became a real threat. citeturn0search21  
- There were reports of malware disguised as extensions on public registries. citeturn0news15  
- Academic audits highlight that autonomous tools with broad permissions carry serious safety risks if not properly contained. citeturn0academia23  

### 👩‍💻 **Developer & Community Chaos**
- Rapid renaming fueled confusion, domain squatting, and scam tokens. citeturn0search12  
- The developer’s GitHub was even temporarily taken over by crypto scammers during the frenzy. citeturn0news16
